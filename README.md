# Traveling Waves with Fractional Laplacian

## Project Overview

This repository contains candidate results on traveling wave solutions for equations involving the fractional Laplacian. The results were obtained by our team through extensive dialogue-based training with AI, following the same workflow as our previous work on the Lane–Emden-type conjecture. The team is currently working to verify all the results.

The study of traveling waves for nonlocal equations driven by the fractional Laplacian is a central topic in nonlinear analysis, with connections to phase transitions, reaction–diffusion systems, and nonlocal dispersal. The fractional Laplacian $(-\Delta)^s$, $0<s<1$, introduces long-range interactions that substantially change the qualitative behavior of solutions compared with the classical Laplacian. This repository documents our progress toward understanding symmetry, cylindrical structure, and related properties of such traveling waves.

## Main Results

### 1. Cylindrical Fractional Traveling Waves

We consider the existence and structure of cylindrically symmetric traveling wave solutions for equations involving the fractional Laplacian. The candidate proof constructs traveling fronts with cylindrical symmetry, in contrast to the planar fronts that arise in the standard local setting. The fractional nature of the operator requires careful handling of the nonlocal interactions across the cylindrical geometry.

**Key features:**

- Cylindrically symmetric traveling fronts for a fractional-order equation are constructed.
- The argument accounts for the nonlocal tail effects generated by the fractional Laplacian in unbounded cylindrical domains.
- The result provides a fractional analogue of the classical cylindrical traveling wave theory.

### 2. Symmetry of Fractional Traveling Waves for $s > 1/2$

We investigate the symmetry properties of traveling wave solutions to equations with the fractional Laplacian in the regime $s > 1/2$. In this range, the operator is “close” to the classical Laplacian, and one expects stronger regularity and symmetry properties to hold. The candidate proof establishes that traveling waves inherit appropriate symmetry under natural nondegeneracy assumptions.

**Key features:**

- Symmetry of traveling waves is proved for the fractional exponent range $s > 1/2$.
- The method exploits the improved regularity available when the fractional order exceeds one half.
- The result is consistent with the classical symmetry theory for local equations, recovered in the limit $s \to 1$.

### 3. Symmetry of Fractional Traveling Waves for $s < 1/2$

We also treat the complementary regime $s < 1/2$, where the fractional Laplacian is more strongly nonlocal and the classical symmetry techniques require substantial modification. The candidate proof develops an approach adapted to the singular, long-range nature of the operator in this range.

**Key features:**

- Symmetry results are obtained for the full fractional range $s < 1/2$.
- The proof handles the enhanced nonlocality and the loss of standard elliptic regularity estimates.
- Together with the $s > 1/2$ case, this provides a symmetry picture across the entire admissible range $0 < s < 1$.

## Proof Approach

The candidate proofs adapt and extend techniques from the nonlocal calculus and the symmetry theory for fractional equations. The main ingredients are:

1. **Cylindrical reduction** – Reduction of the traveling wave problem to a lower-dimensional formulation that retains the essential nonlocal effects of the fractional Laplacian.
2. **Nonlocal maximum principles and comparison** – Use of maximum principles and comparison arguments adapted to the fractional setting, with explicit control of the long-range tail terms.
3. **Regularity bootstrap** – Systematic improvement of regularity for the traveling wave profiles, with separate treatments for $s > 1/2$ and $s < 1/2$.
4. **Symmetry via moving planes or integral identities** – Adaptation of symmetry methods (such as moving plane arguments or integral identities) to the nonlocal framework, accounting for the absence of local differential structure.

For the cylindrical case, the geometry of the domain interacts with the nonlocal kernel and requires a careful localization argument. For the symmetry results, the fractional order $s$ governs the strength of the nonlocality and determines which regularity and compactness tools are available.
